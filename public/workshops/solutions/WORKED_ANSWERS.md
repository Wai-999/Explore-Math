# Worked answers — open after attempting a fresh task

Availability: a known future calendar or approved plan can be eligible; a past event reported after the prediction cutoff is unavailable. A future target or post-outcome repair record is leakage.

Evaluation: future days/weeks require forward evaluation. Unseen-machine deployment requires whole-machine separation; real future inspection also requires mature labels and a temporal cutoff. No universal split ratio solves these design questions.

MAE: sum absolute errors and divide by their count. Errors 20,10,20 give 50/3 = 16.67 packs/week. Signed mean error 0 can coexist with substantial MAE.

Probability policy: with TP=3,FP=2,FN=1,TN=4, precision=0.6, recall=0.75, and cost=12 when miss=10/false alarm=1. Threshold changes can change these values without changing log loss or probability calibration.

A good decision can be to hold deployment. A synthetic score, a tiny reliability bin, or a higher training fit does not establish operating safety. Real data can fail every candidate; collecting better labels or using a simple rule remains a valid outcome.

PCA: fit on training X only; a high-variance direction can be unrelated to y. Reconstruction and orthogonality tests verify the decomposition, not predictive usefulness.

Transfer: a model tested on later dates at existing stores has not been tested on unseen stores. Define the claim first, then design the split to match it.

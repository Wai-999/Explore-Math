# Mathematics and model assumptions

All six worlds are original deterministic synthetic teaching models, version 1. No real customer, market, robot or geographic data is used. They do not establish operational effectiveness or causal parameters. Numerical fixtures and algorithms are machine-checked; no independent human proof review or formal proof-assistant certification is claimed.

## Verification and representation

Each definition supplies its scalar model, analysis, derivation, examples, mission grader and executable Python. Rendering consumes computed vertices/positions instead of reimplementing the model. Default surfaces have 25×25 samples; planar triangles approximate the continuous function. Exact marker/number calculations use the scalar function. Height is rescaled to 0–2.2 display units, with physical units/range disclosed. Coordinate-gradient arrows share one positive scale in native coordinates and are tangent vectors after display mapping; they are not a physical unit-invariant notion of steepest direction. The robot instead draws its actual planar workspace in meters.

Central differences use h=10⁻⁴ in each displayed input unit, with about 10⁻⁷ agreement on the bounded smooth fixtures. Robot position differences are converted from per-degree to per-radian before comparison with J. Kinks are checked separately. Closed-form fixtures generally use 10–12 decimal-digit comparisons. GIS 64-panel costs are compared with 4096-panel references within 0.001 cost units. Graph results are compared with independent simple-path enumeration. Optimization is compared with independent grids including bounds; the grid is evidence for fixtures, not the general proof.

Worked fixtures include Sales `(p,s)=(20,0)` giving q=40, R=800, profit=480 and `(10,2)` giving q=66, profit=122; ML zero weights giving MSE=87/4 and `(2,3)` giving zero training residuals; robot straight tip `(2.4,0)` and `(30°,−30°)` tip `(2.225833…,0.65)`; Finance profits 248 and 412; GIS `f(2,1)=0.8`, gradient `(0.65,0.3)`, direction `(1,2)` rate 1.25 m/unit t.

## Causality, sources and licensing

The Marketing holdout counts are fabricated randomized-example fixtures. A: 105/1000 versus 100/1000, estimated difference 0.5 percentage points, normal-approximation interval crossing zero. B: 120/1000 versus 80/1000, difference 4 points. These illustrate estimands and uncertainty, not a real campaign study. Last-click credits (160 versus 50) are deliberately different quantities. Random assignment, no interference and a valid observation window are assumptions. The worksheet asks for these choices and sample-size caveats; no power guarantee is computed.

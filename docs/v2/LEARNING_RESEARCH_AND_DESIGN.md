## How the sources change this implementation

The following are **author design inferences**, to be tested with learners rather than presented as measured outcomes:

1. **One question and four progressive lenses:** Discover → Understand → Why is it true? → Use it. Keep the active scene in place. Explanation depth changes detail, never the learner's assessed ability or access to other topics.
2. **Correct example, then independent work:** show two checked examples one justified step at a time; let the learner request help, then supply fresh seeded practice. Opening a hint/reveal marks that attempt assisted. New parameters alone do not prove far transfer.
3. **Proof is a different kind of evidence:** state the claim and assumptions, identify each rule, and show a counterexample to an overly strong guess. Numerical agreement and animation are not general proofs. A proof-step question checks that local answer only.
4. **Small explanations instead of essay gates:** ask one relevant “why” question after a meaningful change. Provide a transparent reference/rubric after the attempt. Free text may be saved for self/human review; no invented comprehension score.
5. **Review with honest records:** save activity, seed/version, attempt, objective correctness, help use, and a suggested revisit date. “Independent evidence” means that recorded attempt had no revealed help; it is not certified mastery or demonstrated retention.

## Evidence still required

The owner should arrange 3–5 beginner volunteers for a cold-start vector or derivative task, one unfamiliar transfer, a small saved project, and a later fresh revisit. Record assistance and confusion, not merely completion. A small sample gives qualitative design feedback, not representative effectiveness estimates. Do not invent participants, observations, or improved outcomes. Accessibility, mathematical correctness, software checks, and educational effectiveness are separate evidence categories; automation or an AI proof review cannot replace an independent human review.
